# Viability report — content guide

The rendering is done by `scripts/build_report.py` from `report.json` + `model.json` — as a designed PDF when the output path ends in `.pdf` (HTML + CSS through WeasyPrint, `scripts/build_pdf.py`; the default deliverable — friendly information style: Roboto from `assets/fonts`, team orange / market indigo throughout, the comparison as range bars with the team's number as a dot on the market band, KPI tiles with both values, story cards with icons, three big numbers opening each case, layer pills in the competitive table, step cards for the 90 days, severity pills on risk cards; `scripts/chart_style.py` gives the matplotlib charts the same palette and typeface; `scripts/svg_charts.py` draws the financial picture as inline SVG). An `.html` output path produces the interactive page from the same content: sticky section navigation, series toggles on the financial charts, folded appendix, responsive layout, fonts embedded — one portable file; the PDF is the print of the same HTML and is the version that is paginated and verified or as `.docx`; both go through the same checks and the same pagination verification; this file says what goes into each field. Built-in labels exist for `lang: en | ru`; any heading can be overridden via `labels` (validated: same keys, same shapes).

**The report is a comparison.** The founder brought numbers; the market has numbers; the same business was run on both. Every section serves one question: where do the two disagree, what does the disagreement do to the outcome, and what should the founder go and find out. The report does not say the market is right — it says what has to be true for the founder to be.

**Numbers are generated, not typed.** Every numeric table — KPI tiles, the founder-vs-market comparison, the bridge, unit economics (four cases), outcomes (four cases), the funnel, the comparable benchmark — is produced by `build_report.py` from `model.json`. The author writes interpretation text only. A model number inside prose is a placeholder — `{{cases.market.peak_capital_need|money}}`, `{{lines.Placement.cases.founder.unit_economics.ltv_cac|mult}}`, `{{bridge.0.peak_capital_need.if_founder_right|money}}` — rendered by the builder, so it is bound to a field and a case; the format must fit the field (a % field renders as `pct`, a ratio as `mult`; mismatches are rejected). Any other number anywhere in `report.json` (founder plan, benchmark, evidence figure, experiment threshold, KPI labels, `labels` overrides, the startup name, appendix cells, notes) must be declared in `manual_numbers` with `field`, `source`, `where` — the report paths the number is used in — and `context`: the phrase the number sits in ("10 лидов", "за 90 дней", "€1 500 за полдня"). `context` must appear in the text VERBATIM (it is matched as a substring of the text at the `where` path, with placeholders blanked) and must contain the number in the same spelling — for the text "€99–169" declare the value `169` with the context "€99–169", not "€169"; a value written with a suffix ("300k", "1.5M", "5 тыс.") is declared with that suffix as its value. Digits inside URLs, the row-number column of the evidence table, dates and bare years are exempt. A declaration covers one meaning: the same value in another wording ("10 %") needs its own declaration, so a whitelisted value cannot quietly stand for something else. A number whose source is the model is refused as a manual number — it has to be a placeholder. Dates (02.2025) are exempt. Exceptions: bare 4-digit years with no currency or unit, dates, bare integers below 10. Placeholder formats must fit the field: money fields render only as `money`, percentages as `pct`, ratios as `mult`, months as `months`/`month`/`int`; `text` is for strings and counts. A number glued to a letter (`M36`, `Q4`, `x45`), European thousands (`1.000`) and `5 тыс.` are all numbers; digits followed by a word that is not a unit (`2pr.io`, `1password`) are a name and are exempt. `python scripts/number_audit.py report.json model.json` lists every raw number the builder would reject and the model field that matches it.

**The report is written, not filled in.** `report.json` holds prose — a five-sentence story, one narrative per section, four short paragraphs per key assumption — and the builder adds the exhibits (tables, charts) around it. Five tables stay in the body: the page-1 comparison, the market-case outcomes, the founder-vs-market unit economics, the competitive table (products against the segment's defining features) and the risk table (event / effect on the numbers / early signal / action); everything else is prose with a bold lead. The full tables live in the appendix.

**Page order is fixed by the builder and verified after rendering:** page 1 = title, verdict, the story in five sentences, evidence basis, KPI tiles founder | market, the comparison table (≤ 5 rows ranked by the bridge's effect on capital need); page 2 = the financial picture (landscape): monthly P&L (revenue, gross profit, net — market case with the team's net overlaid, break-even marked), cumulative cash (market and team lines, market-edges band clipped to the main lines, trough annotated as the capital need, cash-back-to-zero marked), capital need as a waterfall (payroll + acquisition + other fixed + upfront − gross profit before the trough = need, the typical round for the funding path as a dashed band) and the signal strip (LTV/CAC < 1 red / < 3 amber, payback > 18 amber, no break-even in horizon red, capital above the typical round red — the same rules of thumb Provify uses); then "What the team believes and what the market shows", "The business on the market's numbers", "If the team is right", "The competitive picture", "The next 90 days", "Risks", appendix. Budget: verdict ≤ 320 characters, story ≤ 900 in total, evidence basis ≤ 180. After the build the .docx is rendered to PDF and the pagination is checked; a failed check deletes the document.

`build_report.py` refuses to build without: a source on every market value and a funnel with a source on every step (from the model input); chart manifests with this model's fingerprint and PNG hashes; a verdict from the three; exactly five story sentences; an assumption story with all four parts for each of the three assumptions with the largest effect on capital need (the top of the bridge); a lead and at least one paragraph for the market case, the founder case and the competition; at least one named player in each competitor layer; 3–5 items in the next 90 days, each with a threshold; at least two risks; the evidence pack; for a multi-line model, `line`.

## Register — the report is a document, not a chat turn

The founder will forward this to a co-founder, a mentor, an investor. None of them saw the conversation. No addressee ("you"), no author voice ("we"), no persona as speaker ("as Lena put it"), no chat connectors ("honestly", "by the way"), no informal evaluation ("hobby", "pretty good"). Conclusion first in every paragraph; verbs of a report (shows, indicates, requires, assumes, exceeds, falls short). Paragraph ≤ 4 sentences, table cell ≤ 25 words. `build_report.py` lints for register and prints every hit; `--strict` fails the build on any. Questions are allowed only as experiment names (`prove[].test`) and in "most important unknown".

## Page 1 — the verdict and the story
`verdict` is one of Proceed / Proceed with conditions / Rethink / Not assessable (rules in SKILL.md § Verdict rules; the builder enforces the Not-assessable rule from `counts.category_estimate`). `conditions` — 2–4 lines, each with a number or placeholder — are required for Proceed with conditions (what has to happen) and Not assessable (what data is needed); they render inside the verdict block. `kill_criterion {metric, threshold, what_model_shows}` is optional and renders under the verdict: the founder's own falsifier from screening, and what the model says at that value.

`verdict_text` (≤ 320 chars): the verdict, then the single biggest reason stated as founder case vs market case, then the funding-path line (market-case capital need against the typical round for the stated path — size only).

**Plain language, not telegraph.** The story and the verdict are read by a founder, not parsed by an analyst. Each sentence is a full sentence with a subject and a verb that can be read aloud: "the business starts earning in month 16 and burns €139k before that", not "плюс на 16-м месяце при €139k капитала". No parameter names (`churn`, `MRR`, "темп новых клиентов") where a plain phrase exists ("how fast clients leave", "how much money comes in each month"); no arrows or "→" in prose; no stacked noun phrases. The 900-character budget is met by dropping clauses, not by compressing them into shorthand.

`story` — exactly five sentences, in this order: (1) what the business is and where it stands; (2) what the team believes and what that gives — with the founder case's break-even and capital as placeholders; (3) what the market shows and what that gives — the market case; (4) which one or two assumptions make the difference and what they are worth (bridge placeholders); (5) what to do next and what to plan on until then. A reader who stops here has the answer.

`evidence_basis` (≤ 180 chars): placeholders into `counts.*` — `{{counts.total|int}}`, `{{counts.observed|int}}`, `{{counts.founder_stated|int}}`, `{{counts.founder_guess|int}}`, `{{counts.unverified|int}}` — so the counts cannot drift from the model.

The KPI tiles (founder | market) and the comparison table are generated. `comparison.notes` may replace the terse source text per domain.

## Measured mode (a live business)
When any founder number is `observed`, the builder switches labels and logic: page 1 is "Where you stand: measured vs the category"; the tiles read "measured | category"; the stories section is "What each metric is worth at the category level", each story's parts being *Measured / Category benchmark / What reaching the category level is worth / How to move it*, with `stakes` bound to `bridge_to_market_by_domain.<metric>.revenue_end.if_at_category` (or `combined.bridge_to_market_by_domain.<line>.<metric>…`); "The business as measured" and "The business at category level" replace the founder / market case sections. The next-90-days items are the metrics with the largest revenue effect. Nothing in the text says "if the team is right".

The tornado (generated): after the bridge, one bar per market assumption at its low and high edge with everything else at the market mid — it shows what the outcome depends on including the rows where the team gave no number. The builder requires a 90-day test for the widest filled-from-market row (team cost excluded).

## What the team believes and what the market shows
`assumptions.lead`: one bold sentence — which assumptions decide the case. Then `assumptions.stories[]`, one per assumption, at least for the three assumptions with the largest effect on capital need (the builder checks the bridge — for a multi-line model the COMBINED bridge, which re-evaluates every line's row on total capital need with shared overhead). Bridge rows do not add up to the founder-minus-market difference (assumptions interact; the builder prints a note), so stories quote each row's own effect and never sum them. Each story has four parts written as prose, each at least forty characters:
- `belief` — what the team assumes and where the number comes from (the plan, a calculation, an analogy).
- `evidence` — what the market or comparables show, with the source named in the sentence.
- `stakes` — what it is worth, as placeholders into **this story's own** bridge row, addressed by name: `bridge_by_domain.<domain>.peak_capital_need.if_founder_right`, or `combined.bridge_by_domain.<line>.<domain>.…` for several lines (numeric `bridge.N` still works but the builder checks that N is this domain's row). A story quoting another assumption's effect is refused. Must contain a placeholder or a number.
- `test` — the observation that settles it, with a numeric threshold and a time.
The builder prints the domain, "X (team) vs Y (market)" and the four parts; stories are ordered by the bridge.

## The business on the market's numbers
`market_case.lead` + paragraphs: how revenue grows, when the business turns positive, how much cash that takes, whether the unit is healthy, what the benchmark edges say about the uncertainty of the market numbers themselves. The builder adds the revenue chart and a compact outcomes table (market, pessimistic / optimistic).

## If the team is right
`founder_case.lead` + paragraphs: what the founder case gives, and what has to be true for it — which assumptions have to hold at once, and which line does not work even on the team's numbers, if any. The builder adds the founder-vs-market unit-economics table.

## The competitive picture
`competition.lead` — one sentence: where the product sits and what that means. Then `dimensions` — the two or three features that define a product in this segment (`key`, `label`, `why`: one sentence on why this feature decides price or choice). Choose them from the research, not from a template: for a done-for-you service it may be "who extracts the founder's thinking", "scope beyond the text", "contract terms"; for a SaaS tool it may be "integration depth", "who pays", "time to first value". Then `rows` — the startup itself (`self: true`, exactly one) plus at least four competitors across all three layers (`layer`: `direct` solves the same job the same way, `indirect` solves it another way, `displacer` could make the product unnecessary). Every row has `name`, `price` (per month or per unit, currency stated), one `cells` entry per dimension (write "no" or "unknown" rather than omit), and `source`. The builder renders one table — product, layer, price, the dimensions, source — with the startup's row highlighted and first, so the reader compares the product against the segment on the features that matter. Then `paragraphs` (≥ 1): the reading of the table, dimension by dimension — where the product is at par, above, below — and `where_we_win`. Prices in the cells are numbers and need `manual_numbers` declarations like any other.

## The next 90 days
`next_90_days[]`, 3–5 items ordered by the bridge: `domain` (the assumption it tests, or `other`), `title`, `what` (why it comes first, what rides on it), `how` (the experiment), `threshold` (a number and a time that say pass or fail). The builder requires a test for the top bridge assumption and for every `unverified` one.

## Risks
A risk is an event outside the assumption ranges or a structural threat — not an unproven assumption (those are tested in the next 90 days) and not a caveat about the report. `risks[]`, at least two, rendered as a table: `title`; `likelihood` (High / Medium / Low); `reversibility` (Reversible / Structural / Unknown); `what` — the event, in one or two sentences; `impact` — what it does to the numbers, with a placeholder into the model or a number (a risk that cannot be priced is stated as "outside the model" with the reason); `signal` — the measurable early sign, with a number and a time; `action` — what is done if the signal fires. Each part is prose, not a fragment.

## Appendix (generated, plus the evidence pack)
A. Assumptions pack — every domain, founder (status), market (source), gap. B. Evidence pack (author). C. Four cases in detail, with the cash and customer charts. D. Unit economics, four cases. E. Funnel and growth. F. Comparable benchmark (month 36 only). G. Bridge, every assumption.
